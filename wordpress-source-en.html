A picturesque, stone-paved slope leading to Kiyomizu-dera, showcasing the timeless charm of Kyoto.

Lined with traditional machiya townhouses, it is perfect for browsing Japanese crafts, enjoying matcha sweets, and finding unique souvenirs.

Highly photogenic and full of atmosphere, it’s a must-visit spot for a classic Kyoto stroll.

Fukuoka Area｜Recommended Nearby Spots

Miyajidake Shrine

One of Fukuoka’s most renowned power spots, famous for its massive
sacred rope (shimenawa) and the spectacular
“Path of Light”—a rare
alignment where the setting sun forms a straight line through the shrine,
visible only twice a year.

Believed to bring good fortune and business
success, it’s also a favorite photogenic shrine for visitors.

Fukuma Beach

One of Fukuoka’s top sunset spots, Fukuma Beach is
lined with stylish seaside cafés and takes on an
especially romantic atmosphere at dusk. With its
open, breezy setting, it’s perfect for a leisurely walk
or sunset photography by the sea.

Miyakojima — Recommended Nearby Spots

Blue Cave (Miyakojima)

The Blue Cave is an underwater cave located off the coast of Irabu
Island in Miyakojima City, Okinawa.
Officially known as the
“Sapphire Cave,” it is a popular spot for snorkeling and diving, famous for its crystal-clear blue waters and magical light reflections.

Irabu Bridge

Irabu Bridge is one of the longest toll-free bridges in Japan.
Simply driving across the bridge offers breathtaking ocean views.

Shimoji Island

Shimoji Island can be accessed by crossing IrabuBridge.
The island is known for its exceptionally clear waters and stunning scenery.
“17END” on Shimoji Island is a popular sightseeing spot, famous for its breathtaking ocean views.

Okinawa Kin (KIN)

This 3LDK accommodation is recommended for large groups.

It features a rooftop with a great view.
You might enjoy stargazing at night.

Kin Town, located near the center of Okinawa Island, has two distinct charms: an exotic atmosphere and rich natural surroundings.

Once a thriving social district, the townscape feels like stepping into an old American town from a movie set.
